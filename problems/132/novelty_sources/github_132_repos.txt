# A conditional convex three-distance theorem for Erdős Problem 132

[![CI](https://github.com/lyfar/erdos-132-convex-k3-lean/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/lyfar/erdos-132-convex-k3-lean/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

This repository is the standalone origin for the Lean Pool project
`LeanPool.Erdos132ConvexK3`. It contains the same Lean modules with a minimal
pinned Lake project around them.

The result is conditional. It does not prove the convex `k = 3` case without
an extra interface, and it does not solve the general Erdős problem.

## Exact theorem

The headline declaration is
`LeanPool.Erdos132ConvexK3.convex_k3_degree_six_of_reduction`.

Consider a finite, nonempty configuration of labelled points in strict convex
position in the Euclidean plane. Join two labels when their squared distance is
one of the three largest realized classes. Lean proves that this graph has a
vertex of degree at most six, assuming
`ConvexTopThreeDraftReductionComplete`.

That single interface supplies the maximal-gap and thirteen-word reduction
together with the route closures. Its remaining source-facing boundary is the
coordinated-majorant selection step used in the proof sketch of Theorem 2.7 of
Erdős, Lovász, and Vesztergombi. The paper does not state that selection step
as a theorem.

## Formalized content

The Lean sources contain:

- the top-three distance graph, strict convexity, and diameter-lens geometry;
- penultimate-coordinate identities and the three-range metric dichotomy;
- the cover process, majorant paths, terminal cages, anti-saturation, and the
  thirteen-word assembly;
- use-site rigidity for the exceptional `(1,2)`, `(2,1)`, and `(2,2)`
  branches; and
- exact rational witnesses, including a convex heptagon that refutes the
  stronger universal longer-cover claim.

The development also records nonnested-pair witnesses that rule out a general
exchange statement. The theorem remains conditional on the one interface
named above.

## Source

Paul Erdős, László Lovász, and Katalin Vesztergombi,
[*On the Graph of Large Distances*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02187746),
*Discrete & Computational Geometry* 4 (1989), 541–549.

[Erdős Problem 132](https://www.erdosproblems.com/132) records the broader
problem.

# A conditional fourteen-point theorem for Erdős Problem 132

[![CI](https://github.com/lyfar/erdos-132-moment-obstruction-lean/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/lyfar/erdos-132-moment-obstruction-lean/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

[Lean Pool PR #272](https://github.com/Vilin97/lean-pool/pull/272). It contains
the same Lean theory as that PR, with a minimal pinned Lake project around it.

The result is conditional. It does not prove the unconditional fourteen-point
case and does not solve the general Erdős problem.

## Exact theorem

The headline declaration is
`LeanPool.Erdos132N14.erdos132_for_fourteen_of_published_inputs`.

For every injectively labelled fourteen-point configuration in the Euclidean
plane, Lean proves that at least two realized distances occur on at most
fourteen unordered pairs, assuming two explicit published interfaces:

1. the published maximum-cardinality bound for planar sets determining at most
   six distances; and
2. the Szöllősi--Östergård classification of thirteen-point planar
   six-distance sets.

The first interface packages the known planar maxima through six distances.
Its exact six-distance case is Wei's theorem `g(6) = 13`.

## What is proved internally

The Lean sources formalize:

- unordered pairs, realized distances, distance multiplicities, and exact
  insertion and deletion identities;
- the Hopf--Pannwitz low-multiplicity diameter consequence at fourteen points,
  via the planar Perles charging argument;
- the hypothetical fourteen-point failure profile
  `(1, 15, 15, 15, 15, 15, 15)`;
- endpoint deletion to a thirteen-point six-distance configuration;
- explicit regular-tridecagon, centered-dodecagon, and centered-hexagram
  templates;
- the tridecagon centroid, isotropy, chord multiplicities, and second and
  fourth distance moments;
- multiplicity elimination of the two nonregular templates; and
- the regular-extension contradiction from the two moments and planar
  Cauchy--Schwarz.

## What is not proved

Two published interfaces remain hypotheses: the proofs of the planar
few-distance cardinality maxima through six distances and the computational
Szöllősi--Östergård classification theorem. That classification-to-template
bridge remains external geometry.

The counting and descent bridge is no longer missing here: Lean derives the
exact failure profile, deletes an endpoint of the unique rare pair, and proves
that the remaining configuration has exactly thirteen points and six
distances. It also formalizes the diameter argument used to start that descent.
The missing bridge to an unconditional theorem is a Lean formalization of the
two published inputs above.

Therefore this repository does not establish the unconditional `n = 14`
case, the all-`n` first clause, or the asymptotic second clause of Erdős
Problem 132.

# Certificates for the E₁₁(5) classification chain

Machine-checked verification of the proof chain behind the classification of
11-point 5-distance sets in the plane — the chain on which Marchetto's n = 11
and n = 12 results for Erdős's distinct-distances multiplicity problem
([Erdős #132](https://www.erdosproblems.com/132)) rest.

Every substantive claim in this repository is backed by an exact, re-runnable
script. Exact arithmetic throughout — rationals, `Q(ζₙ)` cyclotomic fields, or
Gaussian-integer polynomials — with no floating point in any decision path.
Every emptiness result carries a positive control through the identical code
path, so a zero is a result and not a broken search.

## What is verified, and what was found

**Wei, *On 11-point 5-distance sets in the plane*, Ars Combin. 102 (2011)
505–515** proves its classification through Theorem 13 and Lemma 10. A line
audit found the chain carries **five substantive omitted proofs** where the
literature records none:

| gap | Wei's text | status |
|---|---|---|
| Lemma 7, Case 2.1 sub-case (all sides ≤ d₄) | omitted | **proved empty here** — 18,486 label maps, every planar Cayley–Menger ideal unit |
| Lemma 7, Case 2.3 | "we use the similar way" | **proved empty here** — 18,150 maps; two admissible diameter graphs Wei's inventory misses |
| Lemma 7, Case 2.2 parenthetical mirror | dismissed as similar | **proved empty here** — 12,596 maps; the branch was never enumerated by anyone, Wei included |
| Lemma 8, six asserted diameter-graph shapes | asserted | **proved here** — each forces X_D = R₁₁ − 3; the second 2P₃+P₂ orbit is empty |
| Lemma 10 at m = 7, DG(X_D) = C₇ | "we can prove that X_D = R₇" | closed by **Wei–Li–Cong–Gao 2014, Theorem 4** — line-audited below, one genuine defect found and repaired |
| Theorem 13, all six branches | "it is clear that", "Clearly", "similar as in [6]" | **all six certified here** (`theorem13/`) |

**Wei, Li, Cong, Gao, *Distance sets with diameter graph being cycle*,
Taiwanese J. Math. 18(6) (2014) 1981–1990,
[doi:10.11650/tjm.18.2014.4030](https://doi.org/10.11650/tjm.18.2014.4030)** —
Theorem 4 is the external dependency above. Its proof asserts six steps as
"the proof is similar". Line audit (`theorem4-2014/`):

- three of the six are rigorously closed by an exact dihedral-group
  computation (the unique nontrivial stabilizer of an edge in D₇);
- the remaining three decompose into **eight** sub-lines (one, B4, absent from
  every prior inventory); **seven hold exactly**;
- **one fails**: in case E2, when the free edge d(6,7) has d₄-length, the
  intended strict comparison is an **exact equality** on a reachable
  configuration. The value-swap argument the paper relies on does not close
  this leaf. This is a genuine defect in a refereed proof, found by exact
  computation (`theorem4-2014/SUBLINES.md`).

**The repair** (`theorem4-2014/E2-EXCLUSION-codex.md`): a self-contained exact
exclusion replacing the defective step. In the E2 geometry the seven
undetermined diagonals collapse into three algebraic classes; one is forced to
d₂; exact inequalities leave three possible five-distance label assignments;
each yields a polynomial system whose Gröbner basis over **Q** is `[1]` — no
solution over the complex numbers, hence none over the reals. So **the E2
pattern admits no five-distance realization, and Theorem 4's statement
survives its own proof**. An independent second route
(`E2-EXCLUSION-claude.md`, exact Gaussian-integer resultants; corroborative,
not load-bearing) maps the full real configuration space: seven distinct
distances everywhere except three isolated configurations with exactly six —
the bound is sharp, and the theorem is tight against it.

Net effect: with all five gaps closed and the 2014 defect repaired, **Wei's
Lemma 10 and Theorem 13 are discharged outright**, and the E₁₁(5)
classification chain is verified end to end. Downstream, this removes the
unverified-dependency status from Marchetto's n = 11 and n = 12 results,
which cite Theorem 13 directly. Erdős #132 itself remains open.
